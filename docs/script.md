# A Bunch of Rocks: narration script (draft 1, with v3 direction tags)

Narration lines start with `N: ` (spec Q-13). Everything else is a heading or a direction and is not spoken.
Signature lines kept verbatim (D-4) are marked with ★ in the direction line above them.
Voice: Drew (ElevenLabs library, eleven_v3), 'wearily' direction (D-25). Square-bracket tags are v3 audio tags: performed, not spoken.

## Cold open (0:00–0:15)

(no narration: wind; a horizon line draws itself)

## Act 1: Loneliness & discovery (0:15–1:45)

N: [wearily] So. I'm stuck in this desert. Forever, as far as I can tell.
N: I don't know how I got here. I just woke up here one day.
N: [flatly] I'm never hungry. Never thirsty. Never tired. Which sounds nice, until you notice there's nothing to be hungry for.
N: So I walk. Sand. Rocks. Horizon. Then more sand, more rocks, and the same horizon, which has apparently been following me.
N: [dryly] I checked. It goes on forever. Or at least further than I do, and I go on forever.
(beat; day/night cycles speed up)
N: [sighs] Out here, you get a lot of time to think. All of it, really.
N: I started with counting. Then the numbers started having opinions about each other, and that was algebra.
N: A few thousand years later I had calculus. A few thousand after that, quantum mechanics and relativity. I fixed a couple of things along the way.
(★ signature line)
N: It took a lot of thinking. But this place has fewer distractions than a Swiss patent office.

## Act 2: The computer (1:45–3:05)

N: [wearily] One day I started laying rocks in a row. No reason. Just to have made something.
N: Then a second row, below the first. Each rock went where the rocks above it told it to go. One simple rule. Three neighbours in, one rock out.
N: Then a third row. Then a fourth.
N: The pattern never quite repeated. Little shapes drifted across it, bumped into each other, and did… things.
N: Somewhere around the millionth row, I realized what I'd built. With the right rule and enough space, a row of rocks can compute anything that can be computed.
N: It's a computer. Every new row is the next step.
(★ signature line)
N: [flatly] Sure, it's rocks instead of electricity, but it's the same thing. Just slower.
N: [sighs] A lot slower. Adding two numbers takes me about a week. Of walking.
N: So I taught it physics. The real rules, the ones I worked out in the sand. Every particle, a string of rocks. Here. Not here. Here.
N: With enough time and enough rows, I could simulate two whole particles bumping into each other.

## Act 3: Universe (3:05–4:10)

N: [wearily] Which isn't much.
(★ signature line)
N: [pause] But I have infinite time and space.
N: So I added more particles. Then more. Then I stopped counting, which, for me, is saying something.
N: [quietly] I decided to simulate a universe.
(BIG PAUSE ~15 s, no narration: the universe forms, music swells; D-27. Audio is split here in post, no re-voicing.)
N: The eons blur past while I walk down a single row. The rows blur past to compute a single instant.
N: In there, somewhere, the particles clumped into stars. The stars burned down to dust, and the dust clumped into planets.
N: On one of them, something started copying itself. It got good at it.
N: And eventually, it stood up, looked around, and wondered where it all came from.

## Act 4: Our world (4:10–5:05)

(music drops to near silence)
N: [wearily] So if you ever see a mote of dust just… vanish. A tiny flash, and then nothing.
(★ signature line)
N: [pause] [sighs] I'm sorry. I must have misplaced a rock.
N: It happens. It's a big desert. And it's been a few billion years.
(re-take, spoken in the film instead: "It happens. It's a big desert. And a few billion years is a lot of rows." A few billion years is our universe's age, not his time: each of our instants is a row he walks. See RETAKES in rocks/audio/narration.py.)
(long beat)
N: [long pause] Oh. And one more thing.
(★ signature line; cut to the lecture hall, slow clock)
N: If you think the minutes in your morning lecture are taking a long time to pass for you…
(beat; back to him walking a single row)
N: [pause] [sighs] …every one of them is a row I still have to walk.
