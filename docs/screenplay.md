# A Bunch of Rocks

**Screenplay** · animated short film, 5:47 (English and German narration on one picture)

Based on xkcd #505 "A Bunch of Rocks" by Randall Munroe (CC BY-NC 2.5)

---

*How to read this.* Scene headings carry the shot number from `shots.yaml` and the timecode in the film.
All spoken words are one narrator, THE MAN, in voice-over: dry, calm, slightly weary. Parentheticals are the
performance directions given to the voice (`script.md`, next to this file). Lines marked ★ are taken word for word from the comic.
The look throughout: black ink lines that wobble like a pencil drawing, over muted watercolor on paper.
The narration itself lives in `script.md` and the timing in `shots.yaml`; this screenplay describes the current cut.

---

## COLD OPEN

**0.1 · EXT. BLANK PAGE – DAY · 0:00**

A sheet of empty paper. A single ink line draws itself across it from left to right: the horizon. Watercolor
bleeds in, pale sky above, sand below, and with the sand, scattered rocks and, small in the middle of it all,
THE MAN, a stick figure, lying asleep.

The title appears in the sky in handwriting, holds, and fades:

> A BUNCH OF ROCKS

SOUND: desert wind, alone, the only sound.

---

## ACT 1 · LONELINESS & DISCOVERY

**1.1 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 0:15**

No cut: the camera moves down from the wide view and in to THE MAN, asleep on the sand. He sits up, crouches,
gets to his feet. He looks to the right, then back.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(wearily)*
> So. I'm stuck in this desert. Forever, as far as I can tell.
> I don't know how I got here. I just woke up here one day.

**1.2 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 0:28**

Continuous, no cut: the camera drifts a little closer. He lowers his head and stands among the rocks.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(flatly)*
> I'm never hungry. Never thirsty. Never tired. Which sounds nice,

On "nice", a small shrug.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> until you notice there's nothing to be hungry for.

**1.3 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 0:40**

He walks. The rocks slide past, the near ones faster than the far ones. He stops, looks back at the horizon behind
him, and walks on.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> So I walk. Sand. Rocks. Horizon. Then more sand, more rocks, and the same horizon, which has apparently been
> following me.

SOUND: his footsteps in the sand.

**1.4 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 0:55**

The camera pulls back while he walks, until he is a dot among hundreds of rocks.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(dryly)*
> I checked. It goes on forever. Or at least further than I do, and I go on forever.

**1.5 · EXT. DESERT – DAY TO NIGHT, AGAIN AND AGAIN · 1:05**

He sits on the ground. Day turns to night and back, stars come and go, faster and faster.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(sighs)*
> Out here, you get a lot of time to think. All of it, really.

**1.6 · EXT. DESERT, THE SAND AS A SLATE – DAY · 1:11**

A time-lapse. He kneels and writes in the sand: tally marks, then "1 + 1 = 2", then "π = 3.2", then
"a² + b² = c²". Each time he moves to a new spot, the last version of him fades out as the next fades in.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> I started with counting. Then the numbers started having opinions about each other, and that was algebra.

SOUND: soft scratching in the sand for each line.

**1.7 · EXT. DESERT, THE SAND AS A SLATE – DAY · 1:18**

The time-lapse continues, faster: calculus, gravity, relativity, quantum mechanics, until the sand is full of
equations. He crosses out "π = 3.2" and writes "π = 3.14159…" next to it.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> A few thousand years later I had calculus. A few thousand after that, quantum mechanics and relativity.
> I fixed a couple of things along the way.

**1.8 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 1:32**

No cut: he gets up from his last equation and stands among them. Later he walks to a rock lying nearby,
picks it up, and looks at it.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> It took a lot of thinking. ★ But this place has fewer distractions than a Swiss patent office.

## ACT 2 · THE COMPUTER

MUSIC: a slow motif of five notes on soft felt piano begins, very sparse. Over the whole act it adds marimba, a
pulsing low string figure and soft pads, growing patiently. Never loud.

**2.1 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 1:47**

He lays rocks in a row on the sand, one after another.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(wearily)*
> One day I started laying rocks in a row. No reason. Just to have made something.

SOUND: a soft stone click each time a rock is set down.

**2.2 · EXT. DESERT, THE ROWS – DAY · 1:55**

A raised side view: rows of rocks run across the frame, older rows further back. He kneels beside the new row,
touches the three rocks behind the next spot, one by one, then sets a rock down, or leaves the spot empty. Again.
And again. Then he gets up and walks along, laying the rest of the row as he goes. On "a third row", two more rows
sweep in. The rocks follow the real rule: Rule 110.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> Then a second row, below the first. Each rock went where the rocks above it told it to go. One simple rule.
> Three neighbours in, one rock out.
> Then a third row. Then a fourth.

SOUND: stone clicks, exactly where his rocks land.

**2.3 · EXT. DESERT, THE ROWS – DAY · 2:13**

He walks along the front. Behind him, rows appear one after another, further and further back, each one the next
generation of the one in front. Triangles open in the pattern and drift.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> The pattern never quite repeated. Little shapes drifted across it, bumped into each other, and did… things.

**2.4 · EXT. DUNE ABOVE THE FIELD – DAY · 2:22**

From high on a dune: the rows run all the way to the horizon, a vast woven pattern. He is a tiny figure in it,
looking up. The camera slowly pulls back.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> Somewhere around the millionth row, I realized what I'd built. With the right rule and enough space, a row of
> rocks can compute anything that can be computed.

**2.5 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 2:35**

Wide. Endless rows; he walks among them, very small.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> It's a computer. Every new row is the next step.

**2.6 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 2:41**

Close. He bends down and sets one rock at the end of a row. It stays there in every later shot of the rows. He straightens up and, on "Just slower", turns around.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(flatly)*
> ★ Sure, it's rocks instead of electricity, but it's the same thing. Just slower.

**2.7 · EXT. DESERT – DAY AND NIGHT · 2:48**

He walks along the rows. Seven days and seven nights flicker past.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(sighs)*
> A lot slower. Adding two numbers takes me about a week. Of walking.

**2.8 · EXT. DESERT, THE ROWS – DAY · 2:55**

A time-lapse again: he writes physics in the sand, "E = mc²", "F = ma", "S = k ln W", "ΔxΔp ≥ ħ/2". The takes
overlap, so for a moment two or three of him are writing at once, and each take is quicker than the last. On
"Every particle" he looks up at the row behind him. Three rocks glow in turn: here, not here, here.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> So I taught it physics. The real rules, the ones I worked out in the sand. Every particle, a string of rocks.
> Here. Not here. Here.

**2.9 · EXT. DESERT, THE ROWS – DAY · 3:09**

Two tiny sparks hop from rock to rock toward each other along a row. On "bumping" they meet in a small flash and
drift apart. He stands to the side, watching.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> With enough time and enough rows, I could simulate two whole particles bumping into each other.

SOUND: a tiny glassy twinkle as they meet.

---

## ACT 3 · UNIVERSE

MUSIC: the score continues, the same motif, wondering.

**3.1 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 3:17**

He stands by the rows. A shrug.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(wearily)*
> Which isn't much.

**3.2 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 3:20**

Rays of rocks run toward a single vanishing point on the horizon. He stands among them, looking out along them.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(after a pause)*
> ★ But I have infinite time and space.

**3.3 · EXT. THE ROCK FIELD – DAY · 3:25**

The rock field fills the frame. Sparks appear, hopping rock to rock: a few, then more, then the whole field
glitters. He stands at the edge, looking up, and watches it fill.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> So I added more particles. Then more. Then I stopped counting, which, for me, is saying something.

**3.4 · EXT. DESERT – DUSK · 3:36**

Dusk gathers around him as he stands beside the rows. Two seconds of silence before he speaks.

MUSIC: a fragile high string note begins, then a slow crescendo that rises for about ten seconds, like holding
one's breath.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(long pause; softly, with quiet wonder)*
> I decided… to simulate a universe.

**3.5 · THE BIG BANG · 3:42**

No narration. No cut from 3.4: the camera pans left into the rows and onto one rock, which starts to glow until
its light fills the frame. The light gives way to dark paper.
A single point of light. Then, at 3:33, the bang: a warm watercolor bloom, particles flying outward. Gravity and
rotation draw them into a spiral galaxy that turns slowly.

MUSIC: the crescendo arrives exactly on the bang and opens into a warm, luminous bloom of strings, felt piano and
soft wordless choir. Wonder, not triumph. No other sound.

**3.6 · EXT. DESERT, A SINGLE ROW – DAY · 3:55**

He walks down a single row. Far behind him, the rows blur past as they are computed, a flicker of streaks.

MUSIC: the score returns for the eons.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> The eons blur past while I walk down a single row. The rows blur past to compute a single instant.

**3.7 · SPACE · 4:06**

Gas clumps into stars. The stars burn down to puffs of dust. Around one star, the dust clumps into planets.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> In there, somewhere, the particles clumped into stars. The stars burned down to dust, and the dust clumped into
> planets.

**3.8 · A PLANET, THEN ITS SEA · 4:16**

A small watercolor planet, then down into its sea: one cell, two, four, each one pinching in two.

MUSIC: the score's opening motif returns, quiet, as a bookend.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> On one of them, something started copying itself. It got good at it.

**3.9 · EXT. A HILL – NIGHT · 4:23**

A small figure on a hill under the stars crouches, stands up, looks around, and looks up. It keeps looking up for a
long breath. The page fades to blank paper.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> And eventually, it stood up, looked around, and wondered where it all came from.

MUSIC: fades out with the picture.

---

## ACT 4 · OUR WORLD

MUSIC: near silence. A faint, barely-there pad.

**4.1 · INT. A ROOM – DAY · 4:32**

The scene emerges from the blank page. A quiet room; a beam of sunlight falls through a window. Dust motes drift in
the beam. One of them, on "vanish", flashes and is gone.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(wearily)*
> So if you ever see a mote of dust just… vanish. A tiny flash, and then nothing.

SOUND: soft room tone, a distant city hum. The tiny twinkle of the flash.

**4.2 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 4:46**

He holds a rock in his hand, looks down at it, then up.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(after a pause; sighs)*
> ★ I'm sorry. I must have misplaced a rock.

**4.3 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 4:52**

Wide. The endless rows; he is very small among them, still holding the rock.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> It happens. It's a big desert. And a few billion years is a lot of rows.

**4.4 · EXT. DESERT – DAY · 5:03**

He stands by the rows with the rock in his hand, looking away. On "Oh." he turns around and lifts his head.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(long pause)*
> Oh. And one more thing.

**4.5 · INT. LECTURE HALL – MORNING · 5:08**

A lecturer points at a blackboard full of physics. Three students sit at their desks; one slowly sinks toward the
desk. On the wall, a clock: the minute hand doesn't seem to move. The second hand ticks.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> ★ If you think the minutes in your morning lecture are taking a long time to pass for you…

SOUND: room tone, the clock ticking once a second.

**4.6 · EXT. DESERT, A SINGLE ROW – DAY · 5:19**

He walks along a single row of rocks. The camera pulls back, slowly, until the desert is all there is, and the
picture fades to blank paper.

> **THE MAN (V.O.)**
> *(after a pause; sighs)*
> ★ …every one of them is a row I still have to walk.

SOUND: his footsteps; the wind carries on.
